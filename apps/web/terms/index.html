Not for navigation or safety

The site is not a navigation aid. Do not rely on it for navigation, safety-of-life, operational or legal decisions. Observations can be late, missing or wrong, and forecasts and model output are estimates, labeled as such.

On or before going on the water, use official sources: NOAA nautical charts, National Weather Service marine forecasts and warnings, and U.S. Coast Guard broadcasts and Local Notices to Mariners. The decision to go out, and how, rests with you and the vessel's operator. You use the site at your own risk.

The data

The observations, forecasts and model output come from the public agencies and university observing systems below. Each provider's own terms apply to its data; please credit the provider when you use it. The providers do not operate, review or endorse this site. All data are provisional, may contain errors, and may be revised.

  • LISICOS, Department of Marine Sciences, University of Connecticut: buoy observations
  • NOAA CO-OPS: shore stations, water levels, current meters, and tide and tidal current predictions
  • NOAA National Weather Service: HRRR and GFS forecasts, NWPS wave forecasts and marine advisory zones
  • Iowa Environmental Mesonet: National Weather Service airport weather (ASOS)
  • U.S. Geological Survey: river flow, river turbidity and watershed boundaries
  • NOAA NESDIS CoastWatch: chlorophyll-a and water clarity (VIIRS)
  • NASA JPL (MUR, via PO.DAAC and CoastWatch): sea surface temperature
  • NECOFS, UMass Dartmouth (SMAST), credited as NECOFS/UMASSD: modeled currents
  • NOAA Office of Coast Survey: electronic navigational chart features (shown for reference, not for navigation)
  • U.S. Naval Observatory: moon phases
  • OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap contributors: basemap

Use of AI

No AI model produces or changes the numbers on this site. Every value in the plots, readouts and maps comes from the instruments, models and satellite products credited above, through fixed code that averages, quality-checks and converts them as each plot's About explains. No AI system runs on these pages or in the data pipeline, and nothing you type or collect here is sent to one.

We use AI tools to build the site. The code is written with Claude Code (Anthropic) under the direction of a person who reviews every change before it is published; each change, and the tools that helped with it, are recorded in the public history. The page text and method notes are drafted with the same tools, then edited and approved by a person who is responsible for them. References come from the publishers' records (DOIs), not from AI-generated citations. Checks on the data, such as comparing a buoy against an independent record of the same instrument, are run as code, and their results are stated in the method notes.

"Ask your AI" (under Collect) prepares an image and a prompt for an assistant you already use; you copy them and paste them in yourself. The site sends nothing to us or to any AI service. The answer comes from your assistant, not from Long Horizon Observatory, and it can be wrong even with the data in front of it: the prompt asks it to quote only the values provided, and the plots are the reference to check it against.

Mistakes on the site are ours to correct, whatever tools were involved: write to hello@longhorizon.blue or use "Send us a suggestion" under Collect.

Privacy

We collect no data about you. The site has no accounts, advertising, analytics or cookies. It keeps your display choices (such as the theme, units, boat and chosen views) in your browser's local storage, on your device. "Use my location" is off unless you turn it on; your browser then gives your position to the page only, to center the map on your part of the waterway, and it is not sent to us.

Our host, Cloudflare, processes each request, including your IP address, to serve and protect the site. Pages load fonts from Google Fonts, map tiles from OpenFreeMap and, for the 3D views, code from jsDelivr; those services receive your IP address under their own policies. "Send us a suggestion" opens your own email program, and we receive only what you choose to send.
